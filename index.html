Welcome

This site explores British orchestral music written between 1850 and 1950. My focus is on composers whose work has sadly fallen into oblivion. By searching for orchestral scores from various sources I have hopefully created decent renditions of their music using Sibelius and Noteperformer software.

We can at last hear their music brought back to life; music which has for too long been forgotten, and in some cases never performed.

Featured Article

Percy Pitt (1869–1932)

"A life on the podium, a composer in the shadows"

Percy Pitt helped shape British musical life from the concert platform, the opera house and the broadcasting studio. Born in London in 1869, he trained in Leipzig with Reinecke and Jadassohn and continued his studies with Rheinberger in Munich. Returning to London in 1893, he became closely associated with Queen's Hall, working as pianist, organist and accompanist.

His conducting career began in earnest when he was appointed in late 1902 as Music Advisor at the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden — later becoming Director of Music there in 1907. From 1923 he was Musical Director of the British Broadcasting Company, remaining through its transition to the BBC until succeeded by Adrian Boult in 1930. He died in London in 1932.

"His conducting methods were a trifle peculiar. He had a habit of burying his head in the score and waving his arms over his head like a gesticulating stag-beetle..."

Behind this public career was a substantial body of composition. Much of his orchestral music was heard at the Proms and was repeated in following seasons. He was a close, life-long friend of both Henry Wood and Edward Elgar.
